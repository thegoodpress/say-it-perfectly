Why This Speech Is Different

The father of the bride speech carries a weight that no other wedding speech does. You're not just entertaining a room — you're speaking as the person who has known and loved the bride her entire life. That's a gift, and the room will feel it if you get it right.

Unlike the best man, you don't need to be funny. Unlike the groom, you don't need to profess love. What you need to do is speak from the heart about your daughter, welcome your new son or daughter-in-law, and set the emotional tone for the evening.

How Long Should a Father of the Bride Speech Be?

Aim for 4–6 minutes. Shorter than that can feel rushed; longer risks losing the room. A simple rule: if you've been speaking for more than 6 minutes, wrap up.

Father of the Bride Speech Structure

A 5-part structure that works

1
Welcome the guests A brief, warm welcome. Thank people for coming. Set a tone of warmth and gratitude.
2
Talk about your daughter This is the heart of the speech. A childhood memory, what she was like growing up, what makes her who she is today. Be specific — generic praise falls flat.
3
Welcome the partner Speak about your new son or daughter-in-law. When did you know they were right for her? What do you love about them? How do they make her happy?
4
A piece of advice (optional) A sentence or two of genuine wisdom — not clichés, something you actually believe. This lands beautifully if it's personal.
5
The toast End with a toast to the bride and groom. You can also include a toast to absent friends or family if relevant.

Father of the Bride Speech Opening Lines

Warm & simple

"When [Bride] was small, she used to come to me with every problem in the world, certain I could fix it. Today, I'm handing that job over to someone far more qualified — and I couldn't be happier about it."

Gently funny

"I was warned that writing this speech would make me emotional. I said I'd be fine. I was wrong about three paragraphs in. Bear with me."

Memory-led opener

"I remember the day [Bride] was born as if it were yesterday. I had no idea how to be a father. I'm still not entirely sure I've worked it out — but she turned out more than alright despite me, which tells you everything about the kind of person she is."

Example Tribute to the Bride

Example paragraph

"[Bride] has always known who she is. Even as a child, she had this quiet certainty about the world — a kindness that she extended to everyone she met, and a determination to do things properly or not at all. Those qualities haven't changed. If anything, they've grown. And I couldn't be more proud."

Welcoming the Partner

Example paragraph

"[Partner], you have made my daughter happier than I have ever seen her. The way she lights up around you — I noticed it from the very first time she brought you home. Today, you're not just joining our family. You already were."

Tips for Delivering the Speech

  • Practise out loud — reading in your head isn't the same. You need to know where to breathe
  • Print it large — 16pt font minimum. You don't want to squint on the day
  • Pause after laughs — if the room laughs, wait for it to settle before continuing
  • Look up — make eye contact with your daughter at the meaningful moments
  • It's okay to be emotional — the room expects it, and nobody minds a pause to compose yourself

What Not to Say

  • Anything that could embarrass your daughter in front of her new family
  • References to exes, failed relationships, or past heartbreaks
  • Too many inside jokes — remember most of the room won't know the stories
  • "I'm losing a daughter / gaining a son" — unless you can make it feel genuinely fresh
  • Clichéd advice like "never go to bed angry" — be specific to them

Father of the Bride Speech FAQ

How long should a Father of the Bride speech be?

The ideal length for a Father of the Bride speech is between 4 and 6 minutes. This is roughly 600 to 900 words. It's enough time to be heartfelt without losing the guests' attention before the other speeches.

Do I need to be funny in my speech?

No. While a gentle joke can break the ice, the room expects warmth, pride, and sincerity from the Father of the Bride. If you aren't naturally a comedian, don't force it — speaking from the heart is always more effective.

What childhood memories should I include?

Pick one or two specific memories that illustrate a quality your daughter still has today — her kindness, her determination, or her sense of adventure. Specific stories are much more memorable than generic praise.

Should I welcome the partner's family?

Yes. It is traditional and gracious to welcome your new son or daughter-in-law into your family, and to acknowledge their parents and family as well.
