The Best Man Speech: What's Really Expected

The best man speech is one of the most anticipated moments of the entire wedding. You're expected to be funny, heartfelt, and charming — all while keeping it together in front of 100+ people. No pressure.

The good news: there's a reliable structure that works every time, and once you have that, the hard part is done. This guide walks you through exactly how to write a best man speech from scratch, with real example lines you can adapt.

How Long Should a Best Man Speech Be?

Aim for 3–5 minutes — roughly 450–750 words when read at a normal pace. Any shorter and it feels like you didn't try; any longer and you'll start losing the room. The sweet spot is 4 minutes.

The Perfect Best Man Speech Structure

A proven 6-part structure

1
Introduction — who you are Welcome yourself and explain your relationship to the groom. Keep it brief — 30 seconds max.
2
How you know the groom A line or two on how long you've been friends and what makes your friendship special.
3
The funny story (1–2 max) This is what everyone's waiting for. Pick one great story — ideally something that shows his character. Embarrassing, but not humiliating.
4
The moment it changed — meeting the bride Transition from the funny stuff to something warm. Talk about how they met, how he changed, how you knew she was the one.
5
A tribute to the couple A heartfelt moment about what makes them great together. Even if you've been funny all speech, this is where you earn it.
6
The toast End strong. A simple, direct toast to the bride and groom. Everyone should feel it.

Best Man Speech Opening Lines (Examples)

The first 30 seconds set the tone for everything. Here are opening lines that actually work:

Classic opener

"For those who don't know me, I'm [Name] — [Groom]'s best man and, more importantly, the person responsible for getting him here on time today."

Self-deprecating opener

"I've been told that a great best man speech should be like the bride's dress — long enough to cover the essentials, short enough to keep things interesting. I'll aim for somewhere in the middle."

Heartfelt opener

"I've known [Groom] for [X] years, and in that time I've watched him become someone I genuinely admire. I didn't expect to feel emotional writing this. I was wrong."

Funny dictionary-style opener

"The dictionary defines 'best man' as the groom's chief attendant at a wedding. What it doesn't mention is the months of sleep deprivation, speech anxiety, and group chat messages that come with the role."

What to Include in Your Best Man Speech

  • A specific, real story — vague compliments are forgettable; a concrete memory is not
  • A joke that lands even if it doesn't land — misdirection and timing matter more than the punchline
  • Something about the bride — acknowledge her, include her, welcome her to the family
  • A moment of genuine emotion — even one sentence that means it will win the room
  • A clear, confident toast — don't mumble it, make it count

What to Avoid

  • Inside jokes nobody else will understand
  • Anything involving ex-partners — ever
  • Stories involving illegal activity, even "harmless" ones
  • Overly crude humour — read the room
  • A speech that's just a list of adjectives ("he's loyal, funny, kind…")
  • Running over 6 minutes without permission

Best Man Speech Example Closing Lines

Sentimental close

"I've had the privilege of watching [Groom] find the person who makes him the best version of himself. [Bride], thank you for that. Ladies and gentlemen — please raise your glasses to the bride and groom."

Funny-then-heartfelt close

"[Groom], I came today ready to embarrass you. But honestly? Looking at the two of you, I don't have the heart. You deserve every bit of this happiness. To [Bride] and [Groom]."
