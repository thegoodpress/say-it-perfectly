Funny Wedding Speech

Make them laugh without making anyone cringe. Here's the honest guide to writing comedy that actually lands in a room full of strangers.

The Truth About Funny Speeches

Most people who want to give a funny wedding speech make the same mistake: they try to be funny the whole way through. The result is exhausting. The room laughs once or twice, then starts wanting the emotion that's been missing.

The best funny wedding speeches are only about 40% jokes. The rest is warmth, sincerity, and stories — and the jokes land harder because of the contrast. Here's how to do it right.

The 3 Structures That Work for Funny Speeches

1. The Rule of Three

List two true things, then subvert the third for the laugh. This is the most reliable joke structure in speech-writing.

Example

"[Name] is kind, generous, and absolutely terrible at keeping secrets. Which is how I know exactly what he bought [Bride] for their anniversary last year."

2. The Setup and Callback

Mention something in passing early in the speech, then bring it back at the end for a punchline. The room rewards you for the setup pay-off.

Example

Early: "He was always the one with a plan — even if the plan was terrible."

Later: "So when [Bride] asked him how he planned to propose, I wasn't worried. I knew he had a plan. I just hoped it was one of the good ones. Apparently it was."

3. Misdirection

Lead the audience to expect one thing, then deliver something completely different. The surprise is the joke.

Example

"I've known [Groom] for fifteen years. In that time, I've watched him grow into an incredibly mature, reliable, and well-organised man. I'm talking about someone else. [Groom] is still a work in progress. But [Bride] seems to like him anyway."

Timing: The Most Underrated Skill

Comedy in speeches is 50% what you say and 50% how you deliver it. A few timing rules:

The Pause

After a punchline, stop. Don't rush to the next sentence. The pause is where the laugh lives. If you fill it immediately, you kill it.

Deadpan delivery

Don't smile while delivering the joke. Let the audience be the ones who find it funny. Grinning at your own jokes signals that you think it's funny — which makes it less funny.

Build before you subvert

The more convincingly you set up the straight part, the bigger the laugh on the twist. Don't rush the setup.

Funny Opening Lines That Work

Self-aware opener

"I was told I have five minutes. I've been practising for three weeks and I have exactly four minutes and fifty-two seconds of material. So we're going to be fine."

Misdirection opener

"When [Groom] asked me to be his best man, I was honoured, humbled, and honestly a little surprised — I thought he'd ask someone taller."

Dictionary opener (classic)

"The Oxford English Dictionary defines 'best man' as the bridegroom's chief attendant at a wedding. It doesn't mention anything about a speech. I feel misled."

What Doesn't Work (And Why)

  • Jokes about the groom's exes — no matter how funny you think they are, someone in the room will be uncomfortable
  • Long anecdotes without a punchline — if you can't find the joke, the story isn't ready yet
  • Jokes that require context the room doesn't have — brevity and clarity over depth
  • Cruel humour — teasing is fine; punching is not. Know the difference
  • Anything that makes the bride uncomfortable — if in doubt, cut it

The Golden Rule of Funny Speeches

End on something real. After the laughs, the room wants to feel something. Don't finish on a joke — finish on a line that's genuine and warm. The contrast makes both the humour and the heart land harder.

Example closing (after a funny speech)

"I've had a lot of fun at [Groom]'s expense today. But the truth is, he's one of the best people I know — and [Bride], you've made him even better. I don't say that lightly. Ladies and gentlemen, to the bride and groom."

Funny Wedding Speech FAQ

How many jokes should be in a wedding speech?

A good rule of thumb is 40% humour and 60% heart. If you try to be funny for the entire speech, it becomes a stand-up routine rather than a wedding tribute. The most memorable speeches are those that balance laughter with genuine emotion.

What if my jokes don't get a laugh?

Timing is key. After a punchline, pause for a second. If the room doesn't laugh, don't sweat it — just move confidently into the next sentence. Confidence is half the battle in comedy; if you look like you're having fun, the audience will too.

Is self-deprecating humour effective?

Absolutely. Making a joke at your own expense is the fastest way to win over a room of strangers. It shows humility and makes the audience feel more comfortable with you before you start teasing the groom or bride.

What topics should I avoid for comedy?

Never joke about ex-partners, the cost of the wedding, or anything that could genuinely hurt the couple's feelings. Teasing should be affectionate, not cruel. If you're unsure if a joke is too far, it probably is — cut it.
