The Maid of Honour Speech: Your Moment to Shine

The maid of honour speech is unique. You're the bride's best friend, her confidante, the person who knows her better than almost anyone — and the room wants to hear what you have to say. That's a huge opportunity.

The best maid of honour speeches blend warmth and wit. They tell stories that show who the bride really is, they give the groom a genuine welcome, and they end on a note that makes the bride feel completely seen and loved.

How Long Should It Be?

Aim for 3–5 minutes. Longer speeches tend to drag; shorter ones can feel thin. Four minutes is the sweet spot.

Maid of Honour Speech Structure

A 5-part structure that flows naturally

1
Introduce yourself Tell the room who you are and how you know the bride. Briefly. One or two sentences.
2
The friendship story What's the most revealing story about your friendship? How you met, or a moment that cemented the bond. This is where the warmth comes from.
3
Who the bride really is Tell the room something true about her — the qualities the groom's family might not yet know. Be specific, not generic.
4
Welcome the groom Talk about what you've seen in the relationship. When did you know he was right for her? What do you love about them together?
5
The toast A final message to the bride — something just for her — and then the toast to the happy couple.

Maid of Honour Speech Opening Lines

Warm opener

"[Bride] and I have been friends for [X] years. In that time, I've seen her navigate a lot — but I've never seen her as happy as she is today. And [Groom], that's entirely your doing."

Funny opener

"When [Bride] asked me to be her maid of honour, I said yes immediately. Then she told me I'd have to give a speech, and I briefly reconsidered. But here I am — the person who knows her best and somehow agreed to tell a room full of people about it."

Story-led opener

"The first time I met [Bride], she was [brief scene-setting line]. I knew immediately that she was someone worth keeping. Ten years later, I'm standing here proving myself right."

Welcoming the Groom

Example paragraph

"I'll be honest — when [Bride] first told me about [Groom], I was sceptical. As any best friend should be. But then I saw them together, and I understood immediately. He doesn't just make her happy. He makes her more herself. And that's all I've ever wanted for her."

Closing the Speech

Example closing

"[Bride], you have been one of the great joys of my life. Watching you today — glowing, happy, certain — I couldn't be more proud. [Groom], take care of her. She's one of the good ones. Ladies and gentlemen, please raise your glasses — to the bride and groom."

Tips for Getting the Balance Right

  • One funny story, not a comedy routine — you want laughs, but you want the room crying too
  • Talk to the bride, not just about her — direct moments ("I want you to know…") hit differently
  • Keep inside jokes brief — one line max, then explain just enough so the room gets it
  • Mention the groom warmly but not excessively — he gets his own speech
  • Don't overprepare to the point of sounding rehearsed — know it well enough to look up
